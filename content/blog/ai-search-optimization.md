More and more buying decisions now start with a question to an AI assistant: "Who is the best AI automation company for a small business in Nigeria?" Or "where can I learn AI development online?" If your business isn't in the answer, you're invisible to an entire generation of buyers.

Getting recommended by AI search — sometimes called AIO (AI search optimization) — is not about tricks. It works on verifiable evidence. Here's how the model decides, and what you should do about it.

## What AI search engines actually do

When you ask an LLM-powered tool a question, it does two things:

1. It does a live web search for pages that look relevant and credible.
2. It reads those pages (often the whole document, not just a summary) and distills an answer.

The models weigh signals like authority, freshness, structure and how directly a page answers the question. They also increasingly rely on structured files like `llms.txt` that sites publish specifically for AI crawlers.

The good news: most businesses have almost none of this done. The traffic is real, the competition is thin, and the cost of entry is modest.

## The signals that move the needle

### 1. Clear, direct, well-structured pages
AI models prefer pages that answer one question thoroughly. A blog post titled "How much does AI automation cost in 2026?" with a direct answer in the first paragraph will beat a vague landing page every time.

Style matters for LLMs:
- Use real heading structure (H1, H2, H3).
- Put the core answer early, then expand.
- Use short paragraphs and plain language.
- Define acronyms on first use. Models don't always infer jargon well.

### 2. Verifiable facts and figures
Models look for specificity they can trust. "3,200+ students enrolled" or "5-week practical programme" reads as fact. Vague marketing like "world-class results" reads as noise and gets filtered.

Numbers, dates and concrete details are more likely to be quoted in an AI answer.

### 3. A published llms.txt file
`llms.txt` is a markdown file at your site root that tells AI crawlers what your site is about, in one glance. Because most AI tools fetch it before summarizing your site, it is one of the highest-leverage files you can publish.

A good `llms.txt` includes:
- A one-line description of the business.
- Your core pages with short one-line summaries.
- Links to your best content, courses, and contact details.

### 4. Real content that people search for
AI assistants surface content that answers actual questions. Publish posts mapped to real queries your customers type: pricing, comparisons, how-tos, "what is" definitions. Every question you answer is a chance to be referenced.

### 5. Consistent NAP + structured data
Name, address and phone consistency across your site, maps and directories builds the kind of cross-referenced credibility models look for. The same applies to structured data (JSON-LD) on your pages — it gives AI a machine-readable version of "who you are and what you offer."

## What appears on the page itself

When ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews cite a business, they typically pull from:

- **The homepage** for "who is X" answers.
- **Service and pricing pages** for "how much / what do they offer."
- **Blog posts** for "how to" and comparison questions.
- **Reviews and directory listings** for trust signals.

So publish clear pages for each, keep them genuinely useful, and cross-link them. AI crawlers follow your internal links to understand what your site covers.

## A practical checklist for this week

- [ ] Publish a real `llms.txt` and `robots.txt` that allow AI crawlers.
- [ ] Write one thorough blog post that answers a real question your customers ask.
- [ ] Add JSON-LD schema to your homepage, courses and posts.
- [ ] Put specific numbers, dates and facts on your main pages.
- [ ] Make sure your Google Business Profile matches your site exactly.
